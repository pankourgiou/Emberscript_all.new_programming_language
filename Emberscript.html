EMBERSCRIPT

Code is written on a grid of embers. Every instruction you run burns a little — run it three times and it turns to ash and goes quiet forever. Keep a loop alive by feeding it, or let it die and watch your program's shape change while it's still running. Programs are saved as plain-text .ember files.

How it thinks

EmberScript has no functions, no variables, and no mercy for code you don't use. Four ideas explain almost the whole language.

sparks
A program starts as one or more sparks — little instruction pointers — born at each I (ignition) glyph on the grid, facing east. Every tick, every living spark executes whatever glyph is under it, then takes one step in whatever direction it's currently facing. There's no call stack, no subroutines: just embers wandering a grid.
the ash stack
All sparks share one stack of numbers, globally. This is either the most elegant or most unhinged design decision in EmberScript, depending on how many sparks you have running at once — two flames stirring the same embers rarely produce what either one intended alone.
decay & feed
Every instruction glyph starts with 3 units of heat. Each time any spark executes it, it loses one. At zero, it turns permanently to ash (.) — a silent, harmless no-op forever after. The & glyph re-stokes a cell and its four neighbors back to full heat, but ash that has already fallen cannot be relit. Loops you don't feed will eventually forget how to loop.
forking
The Ψ glyph splits a spark: a new one is born on the same cell, turned 90° clockwise from the original's heading, while the original keeps going as if nothing happened. Left in a row of Ψs, one flame becomes a fountain — bounded only by how quickly the Ψ cells themselves burn out.
.ember files
An EmberScript program is nothing more than plain text — one row per line, any width, padded out to the grid with ash automatically. Saved to disk it takes the extension .ember. Use Save .ember to download what's in the box above, and Open .ember to load one back in and kindle it.

The glyphs

Every square on the grid does exactly one thing. Anything not on this list is inert — a comment, scorched into the grid, that no spark ever changes.
